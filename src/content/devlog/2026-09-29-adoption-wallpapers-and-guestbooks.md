Hello, I'm Crafty Olive.

This week's work was mostly about the small things that make Shrimprium feel shared: getting new shrimp, showing your tanks to others and leaving something behind when you visit.

## 1. Adopting from the Shrimpedia

The Shrimpedia, the in-game encyclopedia of every shrimp you've discovered, now lets you adopt shrimp directly, and you can choose whether you get a male or a female.

Adopted shrimp aren't finished, though. They start with their main color at 60 percent, and breeding is what pushes it further. Composite colors, the ones that combine several traits, cost extra to adopt. I wanted adoption to be a starting point for breeding, not a shortcut around it.

To go with this, the challenges now have a TIP window with breeding hints, and each chapter fits on a single screen.

## 2. Your tanks as a live wallpaper

You can now create a link that shows your tanks as a live wallpaper. Anyone with the link can watch without signing in.

The link shows all four of your tank slots side by side as one long tank, with no glass between them. The shrimp swim, climb, dash and perform their tricks just as they do in the game, and they can cross from one tank into the next. The light rays, caustics and gravel were baked across all four tanks at once, so the joins disappear and the patterns don't repeat.

The link is a snapshot of how your tanks looked when you made it, and it doesn't touch your actual save. Creating a new link immediately invalidates the old one, and you can revoke a link at any time.

## 3. Guestbooks and representative shrimp

Published tanks now have a guestbook, so visitors can leave a note for the owner. You can also pick a representative shrimp for each tank, which appears with it in the community list, and your own tanks now show up in the list too, marked as yours.

## 4. Getting ready for Android

Behind the scenes, I also updated the privacy policy to clearly describe the account email and community data the game handles, and added an account deletion page. Both are needed for Google Play, and they are part of the groundwork for an Android release.
