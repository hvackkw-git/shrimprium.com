Every cherry shrimp colour you see in a shop belongs to one species, *Neocaridina davidi*. This family tree shows how the main colour lines relate to the brown wild shrimp they came from. I drew it with the shrimp from Shrimprium.

**The red line.** Breeders in Taiwan and Japan turned the wild shrimp red in the 1990s.[^1][^2] The reds were then graded by how deep and solid the colour is: Red Cherry, Sakura, Fire Red and Painted Fire Red.[^3] These grades aren't separate branches so much as steps along one line, each selected for a stronger red. Around 2010, the Red Rili, a red shrimp with a clear or white patch on its body, appeared in Taiwan.[^4][^5]

**The other colours.** Yellow, orange, blue, green and black shrimp are the same species too.[^6][^7] Where each line first came from is not well documented, so I drew them with dashed lines straight from the wild shrimp.

For the full story of how a brown shrimp became red, read [Cherry shrimp weren't always red](/shrimpedia/2026-10-23-cherry-shrimp-werent-always-red/).
