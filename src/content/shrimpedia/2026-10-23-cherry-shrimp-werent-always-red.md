Hi, I'm Crafty Olive.

I modeled my game, Shrimprium, on cherry shrimp. As the name suggests, the first cherry shrimp were red. But that red isn't a colour nature made. The wild shrimp that cherry shrimp come from are brown.[^1][^2]

The story of shrimp in aquariums starts with a different shrimp. In the early 1980s, Takashi Amano, the Japanese aquarist behind the "Nature Aquarium" style of planted tanks arranged like landscapes, was looking for a shrimp that would eat algae. He found the Yamato shrimp and ordered several thousand for his tanks. The species later came to be known as the Amano shrimp, after him.[^3][^4] From then on, shrimp had a place in aquariums as cleaners that kept the algae down.

Cherry shrimp are *Neocaridina davidi*, a species from slow streams and ponds in East Asia, including Taiwan, southern China, Vietnam and Korea. The French zoologist Bouvier first described it in 1904,[^1][^5] and scientists still debate how it should be classified.[^6] In the wild, a brown or translucent body helps it stay out of sight of predators.

The red was made by people. In the 1990s, breeders in Taiwan, Japan and elsewhere in Asia picked out the slightly redder shrimp and bred them, generation after generation, until they had cherry shrimp. Accounts differ on who did it first and when.[^7][^8] From the late 1990s, cherry shrimp spread to aquariums around the world.[^8][^9]

After that, the colours took off. Even the reds were split into grades by intensity, such as cherry, sakura, fire red and painted red,[^10] and yellow, orange, blue, green and black lines followed.[^2][^5] Around 2010, the "Rili" pattern, in which parts of the body are clear, appeared in Taiwan.[^11][^12] They all look different, but they are all one species.

This colourful history has a shadow. Cherry shrimp released from aquariums have settled in the wild in the United States, Japan, Israel and Europe,[^13] and in Poland a population turned up in canals warmed by power plant discharge water.[^1] Never release your shrimp into the wild.
